# Expert review of the Classes 8–10 AI curriculum

**Reviewed 21 November 2026.** The question is whether a student completing this proposed common school subject would have a sound foundation for further study. The review covers all 18 units in [the syllabus](school/curriculum.md) and all 10 entries in [its source register](school/sources.csv).

## What students learn and how they show it

| Class | Evidence of learning | Judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Pupils sort library messages using both a rule they wrote and labelled examples. In the garden lab they record predictions before seeing the answers. In the notice lab they check three generated claims against a fact sheet. | Tables, word matches and fractions are accessible starting points. Counting deferrals among all cases when reporting unaided success prevents a model from looking better simply by referring hard cases to a person. The individual card trace tests whether each pupil understands the team prototype. |
| 9 | Pupils clean a CSV in a local Python starter, trace a tree, update a cluster centre, and build a word-count classifier. They hand their prototype to a peer and explain a failure using both a count and a rate. | A defensible first programming year. The distinction between learning from labels and finding groups is concrete. The vocabulary comes from training text, and variants of one notice stay in the same split. Both details matter to the credibility of the reported result. |
| 10 | Pupils group related observations before splitting, compare tree depths on validation data, calculate precision and recall, trace a neural unit, and test a model under changed input conditions. The capstone ends with an individual viva and a decision on a supervised pilot. | This makes progress beyond running a supplied model. A pupil must choose which error matters, demonstrate how it was measured and specify when a person should intervene. The word-count exercise models one aspect of text generation, and the separate attention exercise introduces the role of context at a suitable depth. |

**Completeness and time.** All three timetables cover weeks 1–32 once. Class 8 has 32 theory and 32 practical periods. Classes 9 and 10 each have 32 theory and 64 practical periods. At 40 minutes per period, the three years provide 170 hours 40 minutes. Each unit has outcomes, an activity, readings and scored evidence. In each year the five short units earn 50 marks, the capstone earns 25 and the individual assessment earns 25. The component rubrics add up, including the 15 team marks and 10 individual marks in each capstone.

**Fit for a compulsory classroom.** Garden plots, library notices and school-language examples give pupils a way into classification without specialist equipment. Printed or offline activities, shared keyboards, accessible formats and explanations in a school-supported language make participation feasible. Fictional records and teacher-run demonstrations keep pupils' personal information out of the exercises. The timetable does depend on access to a working school computer for each pupil's assessed turn in Classes 9–10. A paper trace supports rehearsal, while the Class 9 practical specifically assesses editing and running code.

**Indian school context.** The blend of data, computer vision, language, ethics, project work and introductory Python is consistent with topics in CBSE's secondary AI materials. The course presents its hours and marks as local design choices. A school adopting it for a particular board would still need to map the timetable, examinations and any separate employability-skills requirements. ## Teaching refinements

- Before the first cleaning script in 9.2, pair §2.3 of *Python for Everybody* with §2.2. A pupil can first trace an assignment, then edit a condition and check the new result. [Author's PDF](https://do1.dr-chuck.com/pythonlearn/EN_us/pythonlearn.pdf)
- In 10.2, give the class a confusion matrix for a majority baseline that never predicts the positive class. Ask pupils to show the zero denominator and report precision as undefined under the classroom convention. This case can arise in the scheduled practical.
- Before week 32, rehearse the shared-device rotation with the actual class size and the local files. Check that each pupil has time to make and run an individual edit. The advanced books and research papers are suitable as the specified short, guided extracts.

The timetable, assessments and linked readings support a teachable progression from checking a claim or a prediction to defending a supervised pilot. The ten sources are identifiable, accurately attributed and available in full for free. The refinements above can be made during lesson preparation.

PASS
